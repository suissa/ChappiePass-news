# Passwordless Identity Briefing

12 October 2026 | Focus: WhatsApp-first authentication, ChappiePass and AllasCode

This week’s most consequential developments are new IETF work on authenticating client instances and presenting credentials to AI agents, alongside a new US government push for post-quantum readiness. No new normative revision of WebAuthn, DPoP or OAuth mTLS was identified.

1. WebAuthn: Level 3 is stable; Level 4 is now in development

On 25 August, the World Wide Web Consortium published WebAuthn Level 3 as a Recommendation. On 15 September, it published the first public Working Draft of Level 4. 

- Architectural impact: Level 3 provides a stable foundation for passkey registration and authentication. Level 4 remains work in progress.
- Recommended follow-up: Keep ChappiePass aligned with WebAuthn Level 3. Enforce challenge, origin, RP ID, signature and user-verification checks on the server.
- Important distinction: A valid passkey assertion proves possession of a credential and, when properly verified, user verification. It does not by itself authorize an Agent to execute an arbitrary Intent.

Sources: [W3C WebAuthn Level 3 Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/news/2026/web-authentication-an-api-for-accessing-public-key-credentials-level-3-is-now-a-w3c-recommendation/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) · [W3C WebAuthn Level 4 draft announcement](https://www.w3.org/news/2026/first-public-working-draft-web-authentication-an-api-for-accessing-public-key-credentials-level-4/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

2. OAuth client attestation: a promising development for Agent identity

The IETF OAuth Working Group's OAuth 2.0 Attestation-Based Client Authentication, draft -11, was published on 3 September and is in Working Group Last Call. It defines key-bound attestation that can establish the authenticity of a client instance without revealing its target audience to the attestation provider. It can also be combined with DPoP. This remains a draft, not a finalized standard. 

Why it matters: Your architecture needs to distinguish the human authorizing an operation from the Agent and client instance executing it.

A useful separation is:

- WebAuthn: human authentication.
- Client attestation: evidence about the client instance.
- DPoP: proof of possession of the key bound to an OAuth token.
- Intent authorization: permission to perform a particular operation.

Follow-up: Evaluate this draft for native clients and trusted Agent runtimes. Do not treat platform attestation as proof of human identity or as a guarantee that a workload remains uncompromised.

Source: [IETF OAuth attestation-based client authentication draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-attestation-based-client-auth/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

3. DPoP: a new draft addresses credential presentation by AI agents

On 28 September, the IETF Datatracker published Presenting Issuer-Signed JWT Credentials to Resource Servers with DPoP. The proposal describes how an Agent can present an issuer-signed JWT credential directly to an HTTP resource server using DPoP, without requiring an interactive credential-presentation flow for every API call. It is an individual Internet-Draft, not an approved standard. 

Why it matters: This closely matches an architecture in which Agents need to demonstrate not only possession of an access token, but also an issuer-backed credential describing their authority or delegated role.

Follow-up: Evaluate whether a credential could represent a constrained delegation such as:

Human → Intent → Delegated Agent → Authorized resource

Require explicit issuer trust, confirmation-key matching, audience and request binding, expiration and revocation/status checks. Do not treat the credential itself as unrestricted authorization.

Source: [IETF DPoP Credential Presentation draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-lee-oauth-dpop-credential-presentation-01?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

4. mTLS: watch emerging session-bound token proposals

RFC 8705 remains the established specification for OAuth client authentication using mutual TLS and certificate-bound tokens. A separate IETF draft, TLS-Session-Bound Access Tokens for OAuth 2.0, explores binding tokens to a specific TLS connection using a TLS exporter value and a signed proof. Its stated motivation includes replay risks in multi-hop delegation and agentic architectures. It is still a draft. 

Why it matters: In a multi-Agent runtime, a stolen token should not automatically be usable by another workload or on another connection.

Follow-up: Continue using mTLS for service-to-service trust. Evaluate session-bound tokens only if the added complexity is justified by your threat model. Account for TLS termination at proxies, connection pooling and token refresh behavior.

Sources: [RFC 8705](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8705/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) · [TLS-session-bound token draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-mw-oauth-tls-session-bound-tokens?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

5. Post-quantum readiness: the migration timeline is becoming more concrete

On 1 October, the National Security Agency announced additional measures for quantum-resistant cryptography. Under the cited US National Security Systems policy, new commercial NSS must support quantum-resistant algorithms starting in 2027, while legacy systems that cannot support them are to be phased out by 2030. These requirements apply to NSS, not automatically to every commercial web application. 

The NIST-finalized standards ML-KEM and ML-DSA remain ready for implementation; the withdrawal of HAWK from consideration does not affect those standards. 

Why it matters: ChappiePass and AllasCode should be able to evolve their cryptographic mechanisms without changing the semantics of identity, authorization or audit events.

Follow-up:

- Inventory algorithms used for signatures, key establishment, TLS, credential storage and token binding.
- Define versioned cryptographic profiles and key-rotation policies.
- Identify where hybrid classical/PQC transport or key establishment can be tested.
- Do not assume existing WebAuthn passkeys or DPoP signatures are automatically post-quantum secure.

Sources: [NSA post-quantum measures, 1 October 2026](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Press-Releases-Statements/Press-Release-View/Article/4615285/nsa-announces-post-quantum-cryptography-measures-to-safeguard-national-security/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) · [NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography project](https://www.nist.gov/pqc?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

6. Passkeys are becoming the default rather than an optional upgrade

Microsoft began rolling out passkeys as the default phishing-resistant authentication experience in Entra ID on 1 September. Microsoft-provided SMS and voice authentication are scheduled to end on 1 February 2027 for the public-cloud service. 

This reinforces a design principle for WhatsApp-first authentication: a phone number or messaging channel should not be the sole proof of identity.

Follow-up: Keep WhatsApp as the interaction and confirmation channel, but separate channel possession from cryptographic authentication. Provide secure recovery for lost devices and revoked credentials without silently downgrading to a weaker authentication method.

Source: [Microsoft Entra passkey transition and timeline](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/13/microsoft-entra-id-security-updates-passkeys-are-the-default-authentication-method-in-entra-id/?trk=public_post_comment-text&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

2 — High	Test that WebAuthn authentication cannot independently authorize an unrestricted Intent.

Architectural conclusion: Preserve the distinction between human authentication, Agent identity, delegated authorization and workload trust. The strongest direction for your system is a verifiable chain from the human's authenticated Intent to the Agent's constrained authority and the resulting Action—not merely a passwordless login followed by a broadly privileged session.
